# Chess History and World Champions

Chess is one of the world's oldest and most enduring strategy games, spanning over 1,500 years of recorded history.

---

## Historical Origins and Evolution

1. **Chaturanga (6th Century AD, India)**: The precursor to chess, featuring pieces representing four arms of the military: infantry (pawns), cavalry (knights), elephants (bishops), and chariots (rooks).
2. **Shatranj (Persia & Islamic World)**: After spreading to Persia and the Arab world, rules were codified. Terms like "Checkmate" derive from the Persian *Shāh Māt* ("the King is helpless").
3. **European Transformation (15th Century)**: Reaching Europe, the game underwent major rule changes around 1475. The Queen and Bishop gained powerful long-range movement capabilities, giving rise to "Mad Queen Chess" and dramatically speeding up gameplay.
4. **First International Tournaments (19th Century)**: London 1851 marked the beginning of modern competitive tournament chess, organized by Howard Staunton.

## Computer Chess and Artificial Intelligence

- **Deep Blue vs. Garry Kasparov (1997)**: IBM's custom supercomputer Deep Blue defeated reigning World Champion Garry Kasparov in a 6-game match, marking the first time a machine defeated a world champion under standard time controls.
- **Stockfish & Traditional Engines**: Rely on handcrafted evaluation functions combined with massively optimized Alpha-Beta search techniques.
- **AlphaZero (2017)**: DeepMind introduced AlphaZero, a deep reinforcement learning system that learned chess from scratch via self-play in 4 hours, soundly defeating Stockfish using Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) and neural evaluations.
- **Modern Hybrid Engines**: Engines like Stockfish now incorporate NNUE (Efficiently Updatable Neural Networks) for state-of-the-art evaluation.
